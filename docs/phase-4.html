04 / Generalization

Validate the method beyond its founding user before broadening the platform.

Version 0.1 · 26 October 2026 · Design proposal · No product implementation yet

Product Blueprint01 · Discover Yourself02 · Career Intelligence03 · Opportunities & Feedback04 · Generalization

01 / Entry gate and central question

Begin after the personal workflow has demonstrated sustained usefulness. The question is whether the interview method discovers meaningful, bounded capabilities outside software engineering, with acceptable effort and reliable corrections.

Do not treat success on the founder’s detailed technical history as evidence that novice users, other professions, or different languages will perform similarly.

02 / Product shape

Introduce lightweight goal selection, profession-neutral interview wording, optional domain context, clear consent and data controls, and a personal workspace. Preserve free narration as the default entry point. Add onboarding explanations only where testing shows confusion.

AreaIntended behavior
Domain contextA teacher, designer, researcher, or career changer can describe an event without selecting a technical taxonomy first.
Model reviewUsers understand the difference between an account, an interpretation, and supporting material.
Personal controlExport, correct, delete, and inspect what goes to an external AI provider.
AccessibilityKeyboard operation, readable evidence views, and list alternatives to graphs.
Public demoSynthetic or explicitly approved examples isolated from private workspaces.

03 / Research and domain adaptation

  1. Recruit a small, explicitly consented pilot with contrasting work contexts; an initial 6–10 participants across at least three domains is a planning hypothesis, not statistical validation.
  2. Collect a short initial narrative and participant-defined goals before showing skill suggestions.
  3. Run the same core interview policy with domain-neutral prompts.
  4. Ask participants to review factual accuracy and interpretation; use domain reviewers for terminology and scope where appropriate.
  5. Separate failures caused by vocabulary from failures of question selection, ownership reasoning, or interface design.
  6. Introduce a domain pack only when repeated evidence justifies it.
  7. Retest on new episodes and new participants; do not evaluate only on examples used to tune prompts.

04 / Domain pack contract

ComponentPurpose / constraint
Concept definitionsPreferred labels, aliases, scope notes, and examples; versioned, not assumed universal.
Probe suggestionsOptional vocabulary and recall cues; cannot bypass ownership and support rules.
Examples and counterexamplesShow what establishes a capability and what remains insufficient.
Normalization mappingsPreserve original user wording alongside canonical English concepts; track uncertain or many-to-many mappings.
Evaluation casesHeld-out examples covering team work, learning, failure, informal experience, and missing artifacts.

Keep the interview policy domain-independent. Never require software-like architecture questions for every profession. Interview language and canonical labels are separate; multilingual support needs explicit evaluation before release.

05 / Data and multi-user foundations

Only introduce multi-user persistence when the pilot needs it. Add explicit workspace ownership to every private aggregate and object-storage path. Enforce access checks server-side for direct lookups, graph queries, exports, background tasks, and retrieval indexes.

Reproducibility must not retain revoked personal evaluation content indefinitely. Prefer consented synthetic fixtures; remove private payloads and derived indexes when required. Document backup retention and restoration behavior.

06 / Technical and operational approach

Retain the modular monolith unless measured constraints justify change. Add authentication and workspace isolation before online multi-user use, not after a public beta. Evaluate database isolation and object access with cross-workspace tests. Background jobs must carry explicit workspace context.

Introduce usage limits, provider cost monitoring, recoverable export jobs, backup restoration checks, and operational support paths. Benchmark realistic graph queries before considering Neo4j or distributed services. Validate provider data handling, hosting region needs, and applicable obligations before external launch.

08 / Evaluation and release gate

  • Have participants verify attribution and domain reviewers inspect capability interpretation; neither alone establishes complete truth.
  • Track unsupported interpretations, ownership errors, corrections, abandonment, interview time, useful discovery, and cost by domain.
  • Test early-career and informal experience so lack of employer artifacts does not become an automatic lack-of-capability finding.
  • Block broader release on unresolved cross-workspace access failures or critical factual attribution failures.
  • Publish the small sample size and limitations; pilot findings do not establish psychometric validity or hiring outcome causality.
  • A successful generalization decision may narrow the audience. Adding every profession is not a required outcome.

09 / Beyond this roadmap

Potential extensions include longitudinal learning, portfolio narratives, and reflective decision support. Consider pricing, integrations, and broader personal development only after users repeatedly return for a clear benefit. Preserve the core distinction: the product discovers and maintains understandable personal evidence; it does not define a person or guarantee career outcomes.

Build from real experiences. Keep the evidence, uncertainty, and room to change.
