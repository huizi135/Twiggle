01 / Entry gate and outcome

Begin after Phase 1 produces source-linked, correctable capabilities. The outcome is a transparent comparison of realistic directions, followed by optional CV narratives built from the same underlying facts.

The first comparison is Backend & Cloud Applications, Real-Time & Connected Systems, and Test Automation & Release Engineering. These labels do not establish demand. Market evidence and user preference must test them.

Do not hide a work-authorization blocker inside a lower capability score. Do not collapse all dimensions into an unexplained fit percentage.

03 / Core user flow

  1. Select a current goal and confirm personal constraints; unknown is an allowed value.
  2. Choose two or three career hypotheses, including a user-proposed alternative.
  3. Review dated market observations and representative requirement snapshots.
  4. Inspect direct, transferable, adjacent, unknown, and missing capability evidence.
  5. Compare options with preference and feasibility kept separate.
  6. Choose a small action: investigate a requirement, explore a company, gather stronger evidence, or learn a specific skill.
  7. Optionally generate a role-specific CV draft and review its claims before export.

04 / Market research method

Use three horizons: roughly ten years for economic structure; three to five years for role and requirement changes; current snapshots for immediate opportunities. Source candidates include FSO, SECO, cantonal statistics, established industry research, and employer job pages. No source integration or historical dataset availability is assumed.

  • Create a source inventory first: access, license, geography, coverage, classification, frequency, methodology, and retention permission.
  • Keep publication date, observation period, and retrieval time distinct.
  • Compare time series only where definitions, scope, and collection are compatible. Log breaks in methodology.
  • A current sample of vacancies is not a historical labor-market trend. Overlapping search categories cannot be treated as disjoint job populations.
  • If historical skill-demand data are unavailable, report the gap and begin prospective snapshot collection; do not reconstruct invented trends.
  • Reserve provider and dataset decisions until access and permitted use are verified.

06 / Matching and narrative rules

LayerReasoning rule
EligibilityEvaluate each explicit constraint independently. Treat uncertain requirements as unknown; do not assume every vacancy sponsors permits.
CapabilityMatch behavior and context before technology keywords; distinguish direct evidence from transfer hypotheses.
PreferenceUse dated contextual observations and current priorities; do not infer preference from capability.
OpportunityUse sourced, geographically relevant observations; expose sample limitations.
NarrativeSelect different supported emphasis for A/B/C. Do not change factual responsibility to fit the vacancy.

A scenario such as improving French is conditional: identify which observed requirements could change from unmet to met. This does not establish a causal increase in offer probability. Work-authorization and legal conclusions require current authoritative verification when used.

07 / Technical approach

Extend the monolith with Market Evidence and Career Strategy modules. Use normalized relational observations and requirement concepts; add background research jobs only where needed. Keep retrieval and extraction separate from interpretation. AI proposes requirement mappings and explanations; the application validates source references and current personal-model revisions.

Suggested operations: register a market source, import reviewed observations, normalize a job snapshot, assess one hypothesis, compare scenarios, draft CV statements, and export a reviewed version. Store source and prompt/model versions for reproducibility. Embedding search is optional retrieval support, not a proof of skill equivalence.

09 / Evaluation and exit gate

  • Use hand-reviewed requirement examples including ambiguous language, preferred credentials, overlapping skills, and location constraints.
  • Test a strong technical fit with an unmet eligibility condition; the result must preserve both findings.
  • Test missing market data and incompatible counts; the system must decline the unsupported comparison.
  • Check all released CV claims for valid source paths and accurate ownership; no invented metrics or completed planned work.
  • Success: the user can explain the differences between career options, the evidence behind them, and one reasonable next experiment.
  • Defer automated applications, broad job crawling, salary prediction, and global occupational coverage.
Build from real experiences. Keep the evidence, uncertainty, and room to change.
